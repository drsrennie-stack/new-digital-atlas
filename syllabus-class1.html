Section 2

Course description and outcomes

BIO 004 studies the structural organization of the human body from cell to organism. You will work in three modes: microscopic study of prepared slides, gross (macroscopic) anatomy, and examination of prosected human material.

This is a pure anatomy course. We are after structure: what a thing is, where it sits, what it looks like, and how it relates to everything packed in around it. What I thread through all of it is clinical context, because a structure you have seen matter to a real patient is a structure you remember.

By the end, you should be able to:

  1. Identify and distinguish tissues using currently recognized criteria.
  2. Describe the microscopic and gross anatomy of the body's major systems.
  3. Identify, in the lab, the major microscopic and gross structures of those systems.

Advisory: BIO 016 and BIO 016L are recommended but not required.

Section 3

How this course works

Each week you'll have the full concept lecture online and a guided worksheet. Class time is where you work through it together and make it stick.

What a class day looks like

  • Most days are guided lecture. We work through the concepts that carry the most weight rather than every detail in the chapter, and you fill in your worksheet as we go. I drop check-in questions into the lecture to see where everyone is, and the students who studied the night before are the ones who do well on them. The full detail lives in the online lecture and in your worksheet, which is why the pre-work matters.
  • Some days, active learning. Instead of lecture, we run activities and games that make the anatomy stick.
  • A few days, TBL. Several times across the term you'll do a Team-Based Learning session covering that module's material (what that is, below).

And lab runs most class days. The online lecture and worksheet are your prep, look them over the night before and class makes far more sense.

What's a TBL?

Team-Based Learning. You take a short readiness quiz on your own first, then take the same quiz again with your team and talk through the answers. It covers that module's material and counts toward your grade, it rewards preparing and learning from your teammates. You keep the same team all term.

The one habit that makes it work: look over the lecture and worksheet before class. Come in prepared and the check questions, activities, TBLs, and lab all click.

Team-Based Learning, 30%

  • 9 TBLs across the term, done with your assigned team on TBL days.
  • iRAT (individual) then tRAT (same questions, with your team). Your individual prep drives the score.
  • Application and case work follow the tRAT, ungraded, but be ready to think out loud.
  • No make-ups. No dropped TBLs.

Lecture exams, 30%

  • 5 exams, one at the close of each module, covering the concept material (the online lectures and in-class recap).
  • Mostly DOK 2–3 (apply and reason), not pure recall.

Lab practical exams, 30%

  • Five practicals, one at the end of each module. You identify structures on slides, models, charts, and prosected material.
  • Timed station rotation. Spelling counts. Pen only (no pencil).
  • The doors lock when the practical starts. If you are late you cannot come in, and there is no second sitting. Keep your answer sheet covered and your eyes on your own station.
  • No make-ups, none dropped.

Lab quizzes, 5%

  • At the end of most labs, one randomly chosen person from each team answers the lab challenge question on that day's material. The whole team earns the point, or doesn't.
  • This rewards everyone contributing, working together, and actually committing the material to memory. If a teammate isn't participating, it is on the team to get them on board.
  • If you are there, you earn whatever your team earned. If you are not, you earn a zero. There are no make-ups and nothing is dropped.

Brain dumps, 5%

  • A brain dump opens most class days. You write down, from memory and on paper, what you took in from the night before's concept lecture and guided notes. No book, no screen, no notes.
  • You will not know in advance which part of the material I ask for, which is the point. Preparing for one piece is not preparing.
  • These are graded on honest effort rather than accuracy. A real attempt earns the credit even when the recall is rough. A few scattered words does not.
  • They are low stakes individually and they add up. They are also the single best predictor I have of how an exam is going to go.

Exam cap: exams include built-in bonus questions, but no exam score counts above 102%. This is the only bonus inside exams, there is no other extra credit besides Scholar Points below.

The workload, honestly

Plan about two hours of study for every hour in class

California community colleges follow the Carnegie standard: for every hour of lecture, expect about two hours of independent study, the classic "three hours of work for one hour of class."

Normally lab hours carry no outside-study expectation, because in a wet lab you run the experiment and you are done. Anatomy lab is not like that. There is no experiment to run. The work of the lab is committing things to memory. Slides, models, relationships, cadaver structures, you have to carry all of it in your head into a timed practical. So here, lab and lecture both earn the three-for-one weight. Both are memory-based.

Here is what that works out to. You are in this room about 8 hours a week between lecture and lab, which sets an expectation of roughly 15 to 17 hours of focused study outside class, so call it 24 hours a week on this one course. I would rather you knew that in August than discovered it in October. Load the early weeks heavily. Anatomy compounds, and so does falling behind in it.

Section 8

Scholar Points (up to +3%)

Scholar Points exist to reward the study behaviors that actually make people succeed in this course, and they are added to your final grade at the end of the term. They are not a way to recover missed work. There are two pieces: up to +2% for putting in verified study hours, and a further +1% on top of that if you also perform well on your exams.

Engagement
+2.0%

36 verified hours

Log 36 verified hours across the term, drawn from three kinds of work: Study With Me, which is what the community study sessions are called here, tutoring (giving it counts the same as getting it), and open lab. Mix them however suits your week, inside two limits: no more than 20 of the 36 hours from any one kind, and no more than 3 hours a week of any one kind. You keep your own log sheet and turn it in at the end of every module.

Performance
+1.0%

80%+ exam average, plus 25 hours

Average above 80% across all ten of your exams, meaning the five lecture exams and the five lab practicals together, and finish at least 25 of the 36 hours by the end of the semester. Both conditions, not one or the other.

This is meant to be reachable. You do not need money or a car to earn it. Tutoring someone else counts the same as being tutored, online counts the same as in person, and hosting a Study With Me session counts the same as attending one. Open lab is free and it is already on your campus. The weekly caps are deliberate: they mean a student working a job or caring for family can get there a few hours at a time, instead of needing a free weekend nobody has. And nothing here stops you from studying however much you want. Once you hit a cap you are welcome to keep going, and I hope you do. It just stops adding to the point total.

New for Fall 2026: Bio101 counts for 20 hours. Bio101, How to Study Science, is a half unit online Solano course, 8 weeks and asynchronous, September 3 to November 1, CRN 80767. Complete it with a passing grade, a P or a letter grade of C or above, and it banks a one-time 20 verified hours toward your 36. The weekly and per-kind caps do not apply to it because it is a single award, and 20 hours is the maximum it can add. To claim it, the Bio101 professor of record emails me directly confirming you completed the course with a passing score, and you check the Bio101 box on your hours log sheet.

You track your own hours. Keep them on the Scholar Points log sheet in Canvas and submit it at the end of every module, not all at once in December. Each entry needs the date, which of the three kinds it was, how long, and who can confirm it. Hours that were never logged cannot be counted later.

No other extra credit. Scholar Points and the in-exam bonus (to 102%) are the only boosts. Please don't ask for more, no exceptions.

Section 10

Attendance and engagement

Anatomy is cumulative and moves fast. Missing class is costly, and most in-class work cannot be recovered.

  • Week 1 is mandatory. Miss a Week 1 session and your seat may go to the waitlist, you may be dropped.
  • Arriving more than 20 minutes late, or leaving more than 20 minutes early, counts as an absence for that session. There is no partial credit for a partial session. Show up, and stay for the whole thing.
  • In-class work cannot be made up. TBLs, exams, lab quizzes, and brain dumps all happen in the room, so being absent earns a zero on whatever ran that day.
  • I treat every absence the same way, whether you would call it excused or not. You never owe me an explanation or a doctor's note, and the academic result is the same either way. Life happens. The material still moves.
  • The doors lock when a lab practical starts. If you are late you cannot come in, and there is no second sitting.
  • If absences pile up I may drop you, which is my call under Solano policy. I will try to reach you before it comes to that.
  • Get into Canvas at least three times a week and turn on announcement notifications, because that is where a schedule change or an exam-coverage note will appear first.

You'll be on a team for the whole term, come prepared, contribute, and answer team messages within 24 hours.

If you decide to leave the course, you have to formally drop it yourself. Do not assume I have done it for you or that it happens on its own. A course you walked away from but never dropped becomes an F you did not need to earn.

Section 11

Academic integrity and AI

Cheating and plagiarism are not tolerated. A violation earns at least a zero on the work, may result in an F for the course, and is reported for disciplinary action. Do your own work, cite what you draw on in MLA or APA, and ask me if you are not sure where the line is. Asking is always the cheaper option.

Using AI

For studying, AI is encouraged, including the "Teach it to an AI" feature in the Mastery OS. For graded work, the rules are strict:

  • Use generative AI only on assignments specifically marked as AI assignments.
  • No AI on exams or on any graded part of a TBL.
  • AI-detection tools may screen submissions. An AI or plagiarism rating above 25% on graded work earns an automatic zero.
  • Unsure whether a tool is allowed on an assignment? Ask before you submit.

See the AI Honor Contract in Canvas for the full requirements.

Section 13

Digital device policy

Device use in the anatomy lab is strictly regulated. We work with cadaveric human donors under a legal agreement with UCSF's body donation program. The people who donated their bodies to your education cannot consent to being photographed or recorded, and we protect their privacy and dignity.

The rules:

  • No phones, smart watches, AI glasses, tablets, laptops, or recording devices in the lab, ever.
  • Devices silenced and fully out of sight: not in a pocket, lap, or on the table.
  • No digital notes, photos, video, or audio in the lab.
  • Access digital materials outside lab, during open study hours or at home.

Violations may mean immediate removal from the course and further action. Students under 18 must sign the policy, and a parent or guardian must also sign, before working in lab.

Read and sign the agreement in Canvas before your first lab.

Section 14

Your team

There are three layers of peer learning, use all three:

  • Your TBL team is your home base, five to seven people, put together from a Week 1 survey so the teams are balanced, and kept the same all term. You take the tRAT together and you are graded on it together, and you work the application problems and cases together too.
  • Study groups, voluntary groups you form to drill structures and run cases outside class.
  • Study With Me sessions are peer-led review that you organize for each other, and the hours count toward Scholar Points.

By the end of the first class, exchange contact info with your team and pick one communication platform. The standing expectation is a 24-hour response time. If your team genuinely isn't working, tell me early through Canvas, before it festers.
